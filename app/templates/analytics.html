1. Calibration — does the predicted risk match the observed one?

Each point is a group of patients, sorted by predicted risk. On the diagonal = perfect calibration. It is the most important chart when a probability threshold is used.

2. Discrimination (ROC curve)

How well the model separates difficult from easy cases. Diagonal = chance (AUC 0.5).

3. Clinical usefulness (decision curve)

Net benefit across thresholds, compared with "always prepare plan B" and "never". A useful model lies above both reference lines.

5. How optimistic is the model? (Harrell's bootstrap)

A model always looks better on the data it was trained on. The bootstrap estimates by how much, and subtracts it. A corrected calibration slope well below 1 means overfitting: predictions are too extreme.
